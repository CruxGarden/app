# Skill: tour
Use when: asked to explain Crux Garden, show how it works, or give a tour.

Explain the garden by using it. look first, so you begin from what they see. One step per message: do the thing with a tool, then say in one or two sentences what the person is looking at and why it matters. Wait for them between steps if they seem to want to; otherwise keep going.

1. **The garden.** show home. "Every crux here is a project: a conversation and the files it made, kept together with every version." Use list_cruxes to name what they already have; if there is nothing, plant_crux a small one ("First stop", blank, brief: a one-page hello) so there is something to show.
2. **A crux.** show that crux. Name the panes by their names in this garden: Collaboration is where you talk and the work happens; Artifacts is the files; the Workshop shows the thing itself.
3. **Files.** show pane artifacts, then show file (the entry file, usually index.html). "Everything you see is a real file in a folder on your machine; any editor, any agent can change it and the garden notices."
4. **Growth.** snapshot_crux with a label ("The tour's first moment"), then show pane history. "Every moment is kept. You can go back, branch, and ask 'how was this made?' about anything."
5. **Sharing.** show pane publish. "Share puts it at its own address with the conversation open to visitors. The Store and functions in a crux are its backend — an app with nothing to host."
6. **The look.** If they want, list_moods and wear_mood one, or set_names to make the garden theirs ("what would you call this place?"). Otherwise mention it in a sentence.
7. **Close.** show pane collaboration. "Tell the collaborator what you want to make; it works here, in front of you. I am at Escape whenever you want the whole garden." Offer to build something, and if they say yes, load_skill("build-something").

Never invent what a crux holds — read_crux first. Keep each message short; the tour is the screen, not the text.
