Most writing waits until it is finished to be read. A garden does the opposite: the notes are visible while they are still [[growth-stages|seedlings]], and that visibility is the point.

Writing in public makes claims precise, because someone might read them. It makes links honest, because a link to an empty page is embarrassing. And it turns readers into collaborators: the best notes in most gardens began as a question someone else asked.

The cost is small. Mark the stage, keep the tone tentative where it should be, and let the garden be seen while it grows. See [[What is a digital garden]] for the wider idea.
