A digital garden is a personal collection of notes kept in public and tended over time. Unlike a blog, it is not ordered by date and it is never finished: a note starts as a seedling, gets revisited, and slowly becomes something evergreen.

Three things make a garden a garden:

1. **Notes, not posts.** Each page is one idea, small enough to keep improving.
2. **Links, not lists.** Notes point at each other with wikilinks; the paths between them are where the thinking happens. See [[Tending notes]].
3. **Growth, not publishing.** Every note carries its stage. See [[growth-stages|Growth stages]].

The metaphor comes from Mike Caulfield's *The Garden and the Stream* and Maggie Appleton's history of digital gardens. Both are worth a seedling of their own.
